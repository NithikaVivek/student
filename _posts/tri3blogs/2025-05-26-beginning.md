# Page 2: Building the Foundation — Taking Initiative During Spring Break

## Creating Momentum Before the Project Began

One of the most meaningful experiences this trimester happened before much of the project had officially started.

During spring break, I spent significant time helping establish the foundation for our onboarding game for incoming freshmen interested in computer science. Instead of waiting for everyone to begin coding, I took initiative to create an early structure that the rest of the class could later build upon.

This experience taught me an important lesson:

**Strong technical projects require momentum.**

In software development, successful systems rarely appear overnight. Someone often needs to take the first step by creating organization, direction, and infrastructure.

Working independently also challenged me to think through uncertainty. Unlike classroom assignments with clear answers, designing an onboarding experience was open-ended. I had to think about:

* What freshmen would need to know
* How to make the experience engaging
* How to create systems that could scale later

Because of this, I became more comfortable making decisions without knowing exactly how everything would turn out.

### Key Takeaway

**Initiative matters in technical work. Building infrastructure early helps teams move faster later.**
