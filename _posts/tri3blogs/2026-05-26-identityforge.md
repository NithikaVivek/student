# Page 3: Identity Forge — Designing for Real Users

## Building More Than Just a Feature

One of my biggest technical contributions was building **Identity Forge**, a level in the onboarding game that helped incoming freshmen create accounts while personalizing their experience.

Students could:

* Create accounts
* Choose avatars
* Select their world experience

At first glance, this may seem like a straightforward feature. However, Identity Forge changed how I approached programming.

Previously, I often focused on technical functionality:

> “How do I make this work?”

Identity Forge pushed me to think differently:

> **“How do I make this intuitive and engaging?”**

Incoming freshmen may feel overwhelmed entering a new environment. Because of this, I wanted the experience to feel welcoming rather than intimidating.

This project taught me that even small design decisions matter. Features like avatar customization may seem minor, but they help users feel ownership over their experience.

Technically, I also improved my understanding of:

* Frontend logic
* User flow
* System transitions
* Interactive debugging

I became more patient when troubleshooting issues and learned to treat bugs as opportunities to understand the system more deeply.

### Key Takeaway

**Good software is not just functional—it is designed with users in mind.**
