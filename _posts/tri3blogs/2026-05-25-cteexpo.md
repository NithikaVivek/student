## Before the Event

Before attending the CTE Expo, I expected it to be an opportunity to present the work we have been doing in Open Coding Society and introduce incoming 8th and 9th graders to the computer science pathway at Del Norte. I expected to answer questions about computer science classes and help students understand what opportunities were available to them through the pathway.

## Curiosity Before Attending

I was curious about how interested incoming students would be in computer science and what parts of our CS pathway game they would find the most engaging. I also wanted to see what other CTE pathways were presenting and how they attracted students to their programs.

## What I Saw

I visited several booths and presentations, including:

Gardening/Agriculture – I learned about how students grow plants and maintain garden systems while learning sustainability and environmental science.
DECA – I saw presentations about business, marketing, entrepreneurship, and leadership opportunities through competitions and projects.
Robotics – Students demonstrated robots they built and programmed, showing how engineering, coding, and problem-solving connect together.
Computer Science – I both participated in and observed presentations about the CS pathway, AI, gamification, and projects students can build.
Drawing and Painting – I explored student artwork and learned about the creative side of visual design and artistic expression.

What was the most interesting thing you saw?
The most interesting thing I saw was how different pathways found creative ways to engage students. I especially enjoyed seeing how robotics combined programming with hands-on building, but I was also very interested in how students responded to our CS pathway game and AI chatbot. Many students really liked the wayfinding aspect where they could find their teams and interact with the AI, which made learning feel more engaging.

## What I Learned

One specific thing I learned is that being very user-focused is important when designing experiences for students. During conversations at the expo and the student panel, we discussed how adding features like progress tracking or score systems could motivate students and make them more engaged in learning computer science.

## What Surprised Me

I was surprised by how excited incoming students were about gamification and AI interaction. Many students were especially interested in the AI chatbot and liked the interactive elements of the game more than I expected.

## What Inspired Me

What inspired me the most was seeing how excited students became when technology was presented in an interactive and approachable way. It showed me that computer science can be much more engaging when people feel involved rather than just listening to information.

## Personal Connection

This connected strongly to my personal interests because I enjoy computer science, AI, and creating projects that help people learn. I am especially interested in building technology that is both useful and engaging, so seeing students respond positively to gamification and AI made me think more about designing user-centered experiences.

## Interaction

**Person or group I learned from:** I talked with incoming 8th and 9th grade students, other student presenters, teachers involved in the pathways, and panel participants during the AI discussion.

I explained the computer science pathway, demonstrated the CS pathway game, and discussed how gamification can make learning more engaging. During the student panel, we talked about AI in computer science, the future of AI, ways to attract incoming students to the pathway, and how students can continue growing their skills in computer science after joining.

## Depth of Experience

This event changed my thinking by helping me realize how important user experience is in education and technology. I learned that students become more motivated when systems are interactive, personalized, and allow them to track progress.

## Connection to CS or Future Goals

This connects directly to my computer science interests and projects because I enjoy building tools that combine AI and education. It also reinforced my interest in designing systems that make learning more engaging and accessible, which aligns with my future goals in computer science and AI.

## Final Reflection

This event was meaningful because I was able to help younger students explore computer science while also learning how to improve educational experiences through feedback and observation. It gave me the opportunity to share my passion for technology and better understand what motivates students to get involved.

## Biggest Takeaway

> Interactive, user-focused experiences—especially those involving gamification and AI—can make students much more excited about learning computer science.
