# Trimester Reflection Blog

## Comparing Myself to the Beginning of the Trimester

At the beginning of this trimester, I had very little experience with Java. I understood the basics conceptually, but building anything substantial with it felt out of reach. By the end of the trimester, I had built a fully functional grading system comparable to platforms like Canvas and Synergy PUSD — something I genuinely would not have thought possible when the trimester started.

Beyond the technical growth, my mindset changed significantly. Before, I would jump straight into code the moment I had an idea. If I could picture it in my head, I would just start building it without much planning or outside input. That approach led to one of my biggest early mistakes this trimester: I built out an entire grading system on the backend before ever sitting down with Mr. Mort to talk through what I was actually making. Because of that, I completely missed the fact that a backend-only system is not user friendly at all. It worked in a technical sense, but there was no real interface anyone could actually use comfortably.

After discussing it with Mr. Mort, everything shifted. We worked through what a good UI would look like on the frontend, and the result was something that could actually be applied across a variety of pages and used by real students. That conversation changed the final product entirely. Now, before I write a single line of code on anything significant, I plan it out first and run it by Mr. Mort to make sure the direction is clear and the end result will actually be useful. That shift — from jumping in immediately to slowing down and collaborating — is probably the most important change in how I work this trimester.

---

## What I Worked On This Trimester

The main project I worked on this trimester was building a grading system for Mr. Mort that was actively used by both CSSE and CSP students. This was not just a small utility — it was a real tool integrated into the classroom experience, which made it feel meaningful to build.

One specific piece I worked on was the "You Pick Two" system for the onboarding adventure. This feature allowed students to choose any two adventures of their choice rather than following a fixed path, which gave them more flexibility and ownership over their onboarding experience. Building this required thinking carefully about how choices would be tracked and how the system would handle different combinations of selections across different students.

Alongside my grading work, I also spent time talking with CSP students about how they implemented microblogging for friend finding on Open Coding Society. Those conversations were genuinely interesting because it pushed me to think beyond just the current state of the feature. We discussed the future of microblogging and what it could look like if Open Coding Society evolved into more of a social platform — something closer to Slack — where you can socialize and collaborate in addition to coding. That vision of OCS as a place for community, not just coursework, felt like an exciting direction worth thinking about seriously.

My team and I also spent a significant amount of time this trimester ideating on what our passion project should be. After a lot of discussion, we landed on creating tools that will help CSSE students learn more effectively. We talked through how to implement this within Mr. Mort's current system and also explored how it could fit into Nora's gamified version. I have started planning that out and am looking forward to building it out further next trimester.

## Challenges I Faced

One of the biggest challenges I faced this trimester was one that seems simple in retrospect but was genuinely difficult at the time: at the beginning of the trimester, I did not even know how to visualize an SQLite database. I could not look at the data, understand the relationships between tables, or debug anything meaningfully at the database level. Now, I can build a full grading system on the backend from scratch — creating tables, managing relationships, handling data persistence, and structuring everything so it works reliably. That growth did not happen overnight, and there were a lot of moments of confusion along the way.

Another major challenge I am still actively working through is integrating my grading system with Mr. Mort's existing system. This is more complex than it might sound. It is not just a technical problem — it is also a structural and organizational one. Mr. Mort's system already has its own architecture, its own conventions, and its own flow. Figuring out exactly where my material fits within that system without breaking what already exists requires a lot of careful thought. I have to understand how the current system is organized well enough to know which parts of my work can plug directly in, which parts need to be adapted, and which parts might need to be restructured entirely.

A key difficulty here is knowing where to place things so that they feel native to the system rather than bolted on. If my grading tools live in the wrong place or communicate with the wrong parts of the backend, it creates confusion for anyone trying to use or maintain the system later. I also have to think about how the current system can build off of mine going forward — not just making them coexist, but making them complement each other so that future development is easier rather than more complicated. That kind of forward-thinking integration is something I am still developing, and it has taught me a lot about what it means to work within an existing codebase rather than starting from scratch.

## What I Improved

The biggest improvements I made this trimester were in my backend skills, my Java knowledge, and my ability to lead and keep a team on track.

When it comes to technical growth, the jump from barely understanding SQLite to building a full grading system on the backend is something I am genuinely proud of. I got much more comfortable working with Java, structuring backend routes, managing data models, and thinking about how information flows through a system. I went from avoiding backend work to being the person on my team most responsible for it, which pushed me to learn quickly and take ownership of things I would have shied away from before.

On the leadership and planning side, I also grew a lot. I became more intentional about keeping my team organized and making sure we were all moving in the same direction. When we were ideating on our passion project, there were a lot of different ideas floating around, and part of my role was helping us converge on something focused and achievable rather than getting lost in ambiguity. I tried to make sure conversations had clear takeaways, that we were checking in with the right people like Mr. Mort before committing to a direction, and that individual pieces of work were connecting into a coherent whole.

Planning became something I genuinely valued rather than something I skipped. Making sure the final product is clear before writing code, looping in feedback early, and thinking about how the work fits into a bigger system — those habits made my contributions this trimester more useful and more sustainable than anything I built by just diving in.
